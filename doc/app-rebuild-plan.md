# App rebuild plan

## Outcome and scope

Turn the browser GUI into a better long-term app for clipping 4–7 h gaming streams into short soundboard MP3s. Review is split into three stages that match how the owner works:

- **Triage**: one unsorted clip at a time, gamepad-first, for fast first-pass decisions (Needs work, Ready, Reject).
- **Polish**: the clip list and waveform editor for fixing titles and timing of keepers (Needs work).
- **Publish**: works through Ready in clip order and exports each clip.

Old `.jipandan.json` sessions are intentionally not converted. Work from them is exported from an older checkout, and new sessions start fresh from the SRT.

### Step 1: event store and Triage

Each recording is stored in `<recording>.jipandan.db`: a clip table with the current state and an append-only event table with every change, decision, and view. Undo comes from the events, so it survives restarts. It replaced `.jipandan.json` and the separate review log. Edits return only the changed clips. Triage shows one unsorted clip at a time, gamepad-first, with autoplay. See [architecture](architecture.md).

### Step 2: previews and Publish

The owner signed off on 2026-10-20 after checking it in the real app on a real recording ([#46](https://github.com/pomodorozhong/jipandan-clip/issues/46)). Previews and exports seek straight to each clip without losing sample accuracy. Publish works through the Ready queue with Ready and Exported lists, an inline export panel, Export all, and export snapshots that flag later edits. Polish opens on Needs work and focuses on titles and timing.

Decisions:

- Publish stays focused on exporting, with light timing fixes only. Heavy timing work belongs in Polish.
- Publish keeps one editable waveform. As is is a play-only render. The candidate shows the original timeline with removed audio shaded, so either boundary can be extended into nearby audio. Showing the recording twice, panning separately, was too much to work with.
- The removed-audio shading comes from FFmpeg's retained PCM rather than a re-estimate, so it matches what is exported.
- Keyboard shortcuts work wherever focus is, except while typing, so a clicked button no longer disables them. See the [focus policy](architecture.md#input).
- The header is one compact bar; session and export paths are in a tooltip on the recording name.
- The UI consistency pass follows the [mockup](mockups/step-2-ui/README.md) the owner approved in [#41](https://github.com/pomodorozhong/jipandan-clip/issues/41): one panel, section header, three button weights, a badge set, and a list-plus-main layout shared by all three stages. Triage gains a segmented progress panel, a bounded Up next queue in Likely keep order, and a view-only waveform with padding around the clip.
- Group 1 and Group 2 are renamed **Needs work** and **Ready** in the UI, since the numbers said nothing about what happens next; stored statuses stay `group1`/`group2` ([#42](https://github.com/pomodorozhong/jipandan-clip/issues/42)).
- Triage decides Needs work, Ready, or Reject; existing Unsure clips return to Unsorted when opened ([#52](https://github.com/pomodorozhong/jipandan-clip/issues/52)).
- Each clip status is listed in one stage: Polish has Needs work, Unsorted, and Rejected tabs, and Publish has Ready and Exported. Five tabs in Polish's narrow list column were too cramped, and Publish can already edit and move Ready and Exported clips.
- The gamepad mapping screen groups actions by task, since one list of every action made controls hard to find.

### Step 3: candidate generation

Generate soundboard-oriented candidates from voice activity detection and word timings instead of raw SRT lines, and evaluate them against the owner's kept clips.

Clip timing findings (2026-10-16, 1,380 retimed keeps on three recordings; details in [#58](https://github.com/pomodorozhong/jipandan-clip/issues/58)):

- Most final edges sit in pauses; only 8–22% cut through a word. Retiming is mostly choosing which pause ends the reaction, not fixing misaligned timestamps.
- Whisper word starts are good start points (71% within 0.25 s), but Whisper word ends trail off (35%). Silero VAD speech ends are the best end points (64%), at about 12 per minute and about 1% of stream length to compute on the CPU.
- Together they put both edges within 0.25 s on 48% of retimed clips (chance 7%), as choices offered in Polish. Moving SRT edges to the nearest point automatically does not help (21% vs 22% for the SRT as is).
- Breeze-ASR-25, a Taiwanese Mandarin fine-tune of Whisper, is not worth a second transcription pass. Its word ends are better than Whisper's but worse than VAD's, it takes 1.5–2 times as long as Whisper, and it left keep/skip ranking unchanged.

How candidates are evaluated (owner decision, 2026-10-20, [#49](https://github.com/pomodorozhong/jipandan-clip/issues/49)):

- **Recordings:** 0511, 0526, and 0614, where reviews are complete enough that unkept clips are real rejections. 0511 was fully listened to; in 0614 only explicit rejections count.
- **Match rule:** a candidate finds a kept clip when it overlaps the kept clip's final exported bounds by at least 80% and both edges fall within an edge tolerance. The tolerance is not fixed yet: 300 ms is likely too strict, so the evaluation script reports results at several tolerances, and the owner picks one after seeing them for SRT lines and the generator.
- **Metrics:** the share of kept clips found (recall), candidates per hour of recording (review cost), and the boundary error a found clip would still need fixed.
- **Target:** the generator beats SRT lines when it has the same or better recall with fewer candidates and smaller edge errors.

First results (2026-10-20, [#47](https://github.com/pomodorozhong/jipandan-clip/issues/47)), tuned on 0602:

- The generator takes each run of voice activity that Whisper heard words in, starting 0.2 s before the speech, plus each Whisper segment with its edges moved to nearby voice-activity edges. Candidates are 0.6–5 s long.
- Against the old SRT lines it misses the target: across 250 ms–1.5 s tolerances it finds 30–57% of kept clips against 41–63%, at 792 against 643 candidates per hour. Its edges are closer (median 0.10–0.16 s start, 0.06–0.08 s end), and on retimed keeps it finds more (27–49% against 13–20% at 250 ms). Keeps that were never retimed match their SRT line by construction, which favours the SRT lines.
- The app's own transcription today is much worse than the old SRT files. On 0614 with the web UI defaults, its lines found 4–25% of kept clips against 48–70% for the generator.
- About 11% of kept clips on 0602 have no detected speech (laughs, shouts), which caps any voice-activity approach.

Decision (owner, 2026-10-20): keep both. Transcription saves transcript lines and generated clips, and the Transcription screen picks which one a new session starts from, with transcript lines as the default. The owner will try generated clips on the next real clipping session, and its review speed decides whether they become the default.

First real session with generated clips (0903, 2026-10-21): 6,071 candidates, reviewed as a review set of 300 (see [Review rounds](#review-rounds)). The set took 77 minutes from start to completion: about 22 minutes in Triage, 24 in Polish, and 29 in Publish. 169 of the 300 set clips were kept, 119 of the 171 exported files got a new title, and 48 had their start trimmed by hand.

Decision (owner, 2026-10-21): transcript lines stay the default. The timing of generated clips felt off and the owner prefers the SRT lines; generated clips stay available on the Transcription screen, and improving their timing is the open part of [#47](https://github.com/pomodorozhong/jipandan-clip/issues/47).

## Ranking direction

Triage orders clips by a "Likely keep" score. The app ships fixed built-in weights rather than training per user. The event log, plus 5% exploration picks, collects unbiased review data for retraining the weights centrally.

Experiments so far reach about 0.79–0.80 mean AUC (leave-one-recording-out) from loudness, playback, and Whisper confidence features. Learned weights on audio, context, and title n-grams gave only a small gain; an LLM rating of whether a line is a general, reusable soundboard reaction was the only clear gain. Experiment scripts and results live in gitignored `tmp/clip-ranking-experiment-*` folders.

Why the app logs review activity ([#36](https://github.com/pomodorozhong/jipandan-clip/pull/36)):

- **The score has hit a ceiling.** In time order, finding 80% of a recording's keepers means reviewing about 80% of its clips; the best models still need about 48%. The features overlap heavily, and better models need better labels.
- **Old labels were weak.** Session files kept only each clip's final status: no record of what was seen, played, or decided in what order. Skip meant both "unusable" and "pass for now", and Unsorted mixed "never reviewed" with "seen but undecided". Recordings were also labelled differently: every clip in 0511 was listened to, but in 0614 only explicit skips count.
- **Ranked review biases labels.** Once review follows Likely keep order, low-ranked clips are rarely seen. In experiments, training on top-ranked or uncertain samples flipped the weights and did worse than the built-in score, and learning per stream never beat it. Hence the logged ranking context, the random exploration picks, and fixed weights retrained centrally rather than per user.

### Review rounds

Decided with the owner on 2026-10-21 as a single **review set** per session, and turned into repeatable **review rounds** on 2026-10-23 after the first real set: reviewing 300 clips at a time worked well, so a recording is now worked through in rounds, one after another. Reviewing every candidate of a 4–7 hour stream takes too long, and reviewing only the top of the ranking would bias the review log (see above), so each round mixes the top of the ranking with a random sample that keeps the data usable for retraining.

- **What a round is.** A fixed number of clips (100, 200, 300, or 500; 300 by default, and the last size used is remembered) taken from the round's **pool**: the clips that are still Unsorted and not in the open round. The highest Likely keep clips of the pool fill the round's top part first, and a share of the round (20% by default) is then drawn at random from the clips below them, regardless of score. Rounds picked before 2026-10-25 drew the random part first, from the whole pool, and filled the top part from what it left (see "How the data can be used"). With fewer clips in the pool than the size, the round takes the whole pool. Membership is by SRT index, so clips split off a round clip belong to it. Clips a round ended early left Unsorted go back to the pool; nobody reviewed them.
- **A finished round keeps what it found.** Ending a round saves the status of each of its clips, and its summary, keepers captured, and labels read those saved statuses: a clip it left Unsorted and that is decided later, in another round or in free review, counts where it was decided, and correcting a clip after its round ended doesn't rewrite that round. Rounds finished before statuses were saved (#103) had them read back once from the review history as of the round's end. A random label stands for one random draw: a clip drawn again after an early end counts once, in the round that decided it, and clips split from it add no labels of their own; it is kept when any part of it was kept, and scored as the clip was first cut.
- **Random share.** Set per round on the rounds home with a slider from 0 to 100% in steps of 5; every round starts at 20%, whatever the last one used. Each round logs its own share and pool size, so changing it between rounds keeps the data usable. The home warns when there are no random clips (nothing can check or improve Likely keep), when there are fewer than 30 or under 10% (the keep-rate estimate gets loose: ±25 points at 15 random clips against ±13 at 60), and, more mildly, above 50% (more time on low-ranked clips, fewer keepers per hour).
- **The rounds home.** Between rounds the app opens on the rounds home instead of a stage: the finished round's summary, the next round's size, random share, and estimates, earlier rounds, and a link to review all clips outside a round (decisions made there never join a later round and don't count toward rounds). **Start round N** works at any time: if loudness is still being measured, the round starts as soon as measuring finishes. It waits rather than ranking on partial scores, because the top part needs every clip's final score.
- **Estimates.** *Keepers captured*: the stream's keepers are the clips already kept (exported, Ready, or Needs work) plus, for every Unsorted clip, its chance of being kept, from a logistic curve fitted to the decided random clips' Likely keep scores. The home shows what earlier rounds captured and what the next round is about to add, the expected keepers among the clips it would pick; since the best-scored clips go first, each round adds less than the one before. Round 1 has no random labels yet, so its share reads "known after this round". *Finish around*: the clock time after the next round at the pace of the last three finished rounds, per decided clip, or 30 seconds per clip until a round has been timed. *Training data*: random labels so far and after the round, against the 250 wanted before refitting, and how closely they pin the stream's keep rate.
- **Time counts only inside a round.** A round's time is the part of each view of its clips that fell between the round's start and end; views from before it started, such as earlier free review, stay in the log but are left out, and a view that runs across either edge counts only its part inside. Ending a round, by hand or after its last clip, first closes the view on screen and waits until the app has stored every view, so the summary has all of the round's time; if that fails, the round stays open and ending it again retries. The open round's details show its time so far and the finish time at that pace. A finished round's summary breaks its time down by stage (time, share, clips viewed, time per clip, and changes, counting only changes made while it was open); at the owner's request this replaced the session-wide **Analyze review time** in Settings on 2026-10-23.
- **The whole app works on the round.** While it is open, Triage, Polish, and Publish (lists, tabs, counts, Export all) show only its clips, and the stage switcher appears; between rounds it is hidden. The top bar names the round under the app name with how many of its clips are exported or rejected, and its bottom edge fills as the round progresses; under 600 px this becomes a full-width strip above the stages. Either opens the round's details, where **End round early…** closes it after a confirmation. Triage hides Likely keep scores so they don't sway decisions or give away the random clips, and the round's random part replaces the usual 5% exploration. Every screen lists the round in one shuffled order drawn from its seed, and Polish's order menu is locked to it. At first the top clips were sorted by score with the random clips placed at random ranks, but in the first real set (0903, 2026-10-21) the owner could tell a random clip by a weaker clip turning up among strong ones. Since every round clip is reviewed anyway, sorting by score gains nothing; a full shuffle gives no position away, and keeping one order across screens means each clip is seen in the same company at every stage.
- **Ending it.** A round clip is processed once it is exported or rejected. When all are, the round ends and the app returns to the rounds home with its summary. Ending is logged with how many round clips were still unprocessed, and cannot be undone.
- **Background work.** Leading-silence detection checks the open round's clips first, in the round's shuffled order, and the rest of the session after them. Its saves don't re-render Publish's preview or hold up Export unless they change the clip on screen.
- **How views are timed.** Each view records the stage it was in (`stage`), and a new view starts when the stage changes, even on the same clip, so Time by stage puts each stretch where it happened. Views from before `stage` was recorded take Polish from their status filter. Settings, Help, Start over's confirmation, and the round's details end the view while they cover the clip; Jump, Merge, and the export dialog are part of reviewing it. Playback in Publish's players counts in the clip's view like in Triage and Polish. Publish also logs `preview_wait` events: how long each candidate or reference preview took from its request until it was ready, failed, or was left, so waiting for a render can be told apart from editing. Views and waits stay queued in the browser until the app confirms them; a failed batch is sent again, and each event's `id` is stored once, so sending again never counts time twice.
- **What is logged.** A `review-round` event with the number, size, pool, total, random share, seed, score version, sampling version (`sampling`: 2 since #104), each random clip's chance (`random_chance`), and the top and random clip IDs, and a `review-round-complete` event. Each decision and view records `review_set` as `top`, `random`, or `outside` (a clip outside the open round in a session that has rounds), `round` with the round's number, and `explore` marks random clips. While a round is open, `order` is `review-round`. The field keeps its `review_set` name so older logs read the same way. A session's review set from before rounds reads as its round 1 (logged as `review-set` and `review-set-complete`), with its pool being every clip.
- **Why 20% random.** For a 300-clip round, 60 random clips estimate a stream's overall keep rate to within about ±13 points, at the cost of the top clips around ranks 241–300. 10% halved the cost but doubled the noise.
- **First set (0903, 2026-10-21).** Top clips were kept at 59% (141 of 240) and random clips at 47% (28 of 60), which puts about 2,800 keepers (roughly 2,100–3,600) among the stream's 6,071 candidates. Likely keep barely separated keepers from rejects: AUC 0.57 on the random clips and 0.52 within the top part, against about 0.79 in the earlier experiments, and 11 of the 24 random clips scoring under 40 were kept. One set is too few to tell whether this stream simply has many usable lines or whether hidden scores change what gets kept.

How the data can be used. Since 2026-10-25 (#104) every round clip has a known chance of being picked, saved with the round: top clips were certain, and each pool clip below the top part had the round's `random_chance`, `random count ÷ (pool size − top count)`. Weighting each decision by the inverse of that chance (inverse propensity weighting) makes the round stand for its pool. A round's pool is the clips no earlier round decided, so later rounds stand for what is left of the stream, not the whole of it; estimates for the whole stream add the clips already kept to the estimate for what is left, as the rounds home does. The keep curve itself uses only random labels: each is a uniform sample of what was left below the top part, which is where the Unsorted clips it estimates are. Rounds picked before (the first 0903 set, saved with no `sampling` field) drew 60 random clips from all 6,071 first, so a top clip's chance was below 1 whenever the draw took better clips, and their random clips can't be told apart from clips that would have been taken anyway; their random labels are still a uniform sample of their pool, but weighting top clips as certain would overstate the keep rate (in a ten-clip example with two keepers, 30% instead of 20%). One round is enough to estimate the stream's keep rate and how many keepers are left unreviewed, and gives a noisy check of how well Likely keep ranks. Refitting the score's few weights should wait for about 4–5 rounds (roughly 250–300 random labels), checked on a held-out stream; fitting per stream or adding many features overfits, as the experiments above found. This only holds if every round clip is decided rather than passed, random clips are judged like any other, and clips outside the round are left Unsorted rather than bulk-rejected.

### Round files

Decided with the owner on 2026-10-23. A session database may not survive a future update, so each finished round also leaves a standalone file that a fresh project folder of the same recording can read back, to skip the clips the round covered and keep its decisions for the estimates.

- **One file per round, written once.** When a round ends, normally or early, the app writes `<recording>.round-NN.json` beside the recording (`-2`, `-3`, … if the name is taken) and never changes it again. Rounds finished before round files existed, such as a review set, can write theirs with **Save** in Earlier rounds. The file can be moved anywhere.
- **Facts, not conclusions** (#107, 2026-10-25). Since version 2 a round file holds the round's part of the review history rather than what was concluded from it: the recording's name, duration, and SHA-256 of its audio (kept in the session until the audio file changes); the round's number, size, pool, total, random share, seed, score version, sampling version and random clips' chance (see "How the data can be used"), when it started and ended, its roles by SRT index, where its clips came from (transcript lines or generated clips, the SRT's hash, the measurement version), and where in the history it starts and ends (`start_seq`, `end_seq`); every round clip as the database stored it when the round started, with its Likely keep score; each change to the round's clips while it was open, with the clips as they were right after it and the changes it undid; and every view and preview wait on them. A round clip is any clip of an SRT index in the round, so clips split off later belong to it. Export paths keep only the file name. The owner chose this over saving conclusions (each clip's outcome, final range, title) because what is learned from rounds will keep changing: a new way of reading a round is new reader code, and every round ever saved supports it. What the round found (outcomes as it ended: `kept` for exported, Ready, or Needs work; `rejected`; or `unfinished`; time per stage, counting only the part of each view inside the round; whether a decision flipped between kept and rejected; which changes were undone) is worked out when the file is read, by `RoundHistory` in `round_files.py`, which import, the rounds home, and datasets share. The file is written only after the browser has delivered every view, so its time matches the round's summary. Measurements are saved without history, so a clip's snapshot holds the values stored at that point.
- **Update files.** A change to a finished round's clips (a status, range, title, or export) would be lost from its file, so it goes into an update file beside it, `<recording>.round-NN.update-NN.json`, holding the round's changes and views since its last file, in the same form. Update files are written for every earlier round with such changes when a round ends, before **Start over** replaces the session, and on **Save** in Earlier rounds, where a row shows how many changes are not saved yet. A round counts what it found while it was open, so its outcomes stay as it ended; update files give datasets the clips' later approved state. A round imported from another project gets no update files here: its corrections belong to the project that reviewed it.
- **Versions.** A reader ignores fields it doesn't know and reads missing ones as None, so adding a field keeps the version. The version goes up only when the file's own structure changes, with one function in `UPGRADES` turning the previous version into the current one, and a frozen example of every version in `tests/fixtures/round-files/` that the tests read. Version 1 files (before #107) held each clip's outcome and time by time range; they read as the history that would produce them, with stand-in clip IDs, one synthetic change giving each clip its outcome, and one synthetic view per stage holding its time. A round whose only file is version 1 shows *Older format* in Earlier rounds; **Save** writes a version 2 file from the database as it was when the round ended, and of several copies of one round (by seed), import and datasets use the newest format.
- **Importing.** **Import round files…** on the rounds home reads one or more files. A file is skipped if it is not a round file, comes from a different recording (by hash), is an update file, or is already here (by seed). Its clips are matched to clips here by time, each clip here taking the file clip it overlaps most by at least half of the shorter of the two, so a project cut into different clips (transcript lines instead of generated clips) still finds them; file clips with no match are counted. A clip an earlier round left unfinished can still be matched by a later file, as it would be picked by a later round. Each file clip was one decision and counts once (#103): its outcome goes to the clip here that overlaps it most, and other clips here cut from the same file clip are only covered, left out of later rounds without counting as keepers. The round's counts and random labels come from the file's clips with the file's scores, not from the clips here. The file becomes a finished round here: its kept and rejected clips are left out of later rounds and count toward the estimates and training data, its unfinished clips stay in the pool, and the clips here stay Unsorted, since exports and titles are not carried over. Its time comes from the file, but its summary has no time by stage, since the views and changes behind it stayed in the other project's log. Importing is refused while a round is open, and is logged as a `review-round-import` event.

## Owner workflow assumptions

- Needs work (stored as `group1`) = keep, but title or timing still needs fixing; Ready (`group2`) = ready to export; Reject = unusable; Unsorted = not reviewed.
- Triage is done with a gamepad, Polish with both gamepad and keyboard+mouse, Publish mainly with keyboard+mouse. In Publish the owner mostly fixes titles, sometimes timing, and changes thresholds about twice a session.
